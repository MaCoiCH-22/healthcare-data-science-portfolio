Reading the comparison

The calculation compares how often each reaction appears in reports that list ibuprofen with how often it appears in reports that do not.

What the data shows

For both terms in this query, the reporting odds ratios were higher in 2024 than in 2023. The underlying reports cannot tell us how many people took the drug.

Methods and implementation
  1. openFDA aggregate queries
  2. Frozen counts and query URLs
  3. 2×2 report tables
  4. Reporting odds and intervals

Separate API totals identify reports with an indexed ibuprofen generic name, the selected reaction term, both, or neither. The ratio compares reaction reporting odds in matching and nonmatching reports. Receipt year is the year the report reached FDA. Report-level co-mention can involve multiple drugs and reactions.

Limitations

People take ibuprofen to treat headaches. A headache listed in a report may reflect the reason for taking ibuprofen, which can inflate the headache association. Spontaneous reports lack an exposed population denominator. Duplicate reports, indication, co-medications, reporting incentives, and uneven drug-name mapping can shift the ratio. These results cannot establish incidence, causality, or a change in medication risk.

Follow-up analysis

A safety reviewer could evaluate whether the pattern persists after restricting to primary suspect drugs, retaining the latest case version, or using an active comparator. That requires case-level data and a predeclared review protocol.
